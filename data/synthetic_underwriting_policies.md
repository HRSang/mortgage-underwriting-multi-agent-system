# Synthetic Mortgage Underwriting Policies

These fictional policies exist only to demonstrate retrieval-augmented generation. They are simplified examples, not legal, regulatory, or investor guidance.

## 1.1 Credit Score Review

Credit scores of 740 or higher generally indicate lower credit risk in this demonstration. Scores from 680 through 739 require review of recent delinquencies, revolving utilization, and compensating factors. Scores from 620 through 679 require enhanced review and may require additional conditions. Scores below 620 are outside the standard demonstration policy.

The analyst must also review bankruptcies, foreclosures, collections, recent inquiries, and payment history. A score alone must not determine the recommendation.

## 1.2 Derogatory Credit

Recent bankruptcy, foreclosure, or repeated late payments must be treated as material risk factors. The analyst should identify the event date, disposition, waiting period, documented explanation, and evidence of re-established credit. Unresolved collections must be documented and included in the risk assessment.

## 2.1 Employment Stability

Stable, verifiable income is required. The analyst should review employment type, current tenure, employment gaps, changes in occupation, and the likelihood that income will continue. Short tenure or unexplained gaps require additional documentation.

## 2.2 Variable and Self-Employment Income

Bonus, commission, contract, and self-employment income must be evaluated for history, stability, and likelihood of continuance. Declining or volatile income requires a conservative assessment. Income that cannot be verified must not be used to qualify.

## 2.3 Debt-to-Income Ratio

The back-end debt-to-income ratio includes recurring monthly obligations and the proposed housing payment. Ratios at or below 43 percent are generally acceptable in this demonstration. Ratios above 43 percent require compensating factors and additional review. Ratios above 50 percent are outside standard policy unless a documented exception is approved by a human reviewer.

## 3.1 Funds to Close

Verified liquid assets must cover the down payment, closing costs, and required reserves. The analyst must not count funds that are unavailable, unverified, or ineligible under the applicable program.

## 3.2 Reserves

At least two months of the proposed housing payment should remain after closing unless a scenario specifies a different requirement. Retirement assets may be considered only when accessible and appropriately discounted.

## 3.3 Large Deposits

A recent deposit greater than 50 percent of verified monthly income requires a documented source. Unexplained or ineligible deposits must be excluded from available funds.

## 4.1 Loan-to-Value Ratio

Loan-to-value is calculated using the loan amount and the lower of the purchase price or appraised value when applicable. Ratios above 80 percent may require mortgage insurance or another documented risk mitigant. Ratios above 97 percent are outside this demonstration's standard policy.

## 4.2 Property Condition

The collateral must be safe, sound, and suitable for occupancy. Material structural, roof, electrical, plumbing, or HVAC defects require repair, escrow, specialist inspection, or another documented resolution before closing.

## 5.1 Fair and Consistent Treatment

Recommendations must be based on verified financial and collateral information. Protected characteristics and proxies for protected characteristics must not influence analysis, conditions, pricing, or recommendations.

## 5.2 Human Review

A human reviewer is required when policy exceptions, unresolved bias alerts, material data conflicts, unsupported model conclusions, or elevated aggregate risk are present. The reviewer must document the final disposition and rationale.
